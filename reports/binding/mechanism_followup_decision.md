# Decision after fresh ownership and common-final-query controls

Both GPU studies completed and passed their full audits: 7,776 baseline responses and 96,768 intervention forwards. Every baseline parsed. All 234 regression tests pass. Frozen configurations, source, preflights and job dependencies are recorded in `artifacts/binding/mechanism_followup/`; the [registered plan](mechanism_followup_plan.md) and complete [ownership](value_owner_results_v1.md) and [cue-control](cue_control_results_v1.md) reports preserve every condition.

The ownership study does not establish selective transport at the nominated copy sites. The timing control substantially reduces the aggregate early/late gap, making a tracking-versus-reconstruction interpretation of the earlier timing contrast insufficient. Neither result opens the original final/extrapolation outcomes or supersedes the failed computed-program gates.

## Fresh numeric value ownership

The 36 ordered value pairs exclude both orientations of every earlier literal pair. The individual digits and program structures remain familiar; this is a new pairing control, not structural generalization. There are 1,728 main prompts and 864 wrong-version donor controls across all three instructions, both lexical forms, both quantity queries, identity/swap mappings and canonical/reordered assignments. Main baselines are correct on 1,458/1,728 prompts; the complete baseline cohort scores 2,224/2,592.

The primary raw block 8 was nominated from the preceding study. These are semantic assignment-substitution targets, compared with the same-batch unpatched replay:

| Block-8 intervention | Target correct before | Target correct after | Cases |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First-copy substitution | 758 | 763 | 1,728 |
| Second-copy substitution | 970 | 971 | 1,728 |
| Both-copy substitution / donor answer | 270 | 289 | 1,728 |
| Wrong-version directional control | 1,026 | 1,019 | 1,728 |

The joint patch increases donor-answer adoption by only 19/1,728 responses, or 1.10 percentage points. Already-wrong baseline answers account for 270 donor-consistent responses before patching. Paired-query donor-map correctness changes from 16/864 to 24/864, while paired correctness for the original recipient map decreases from 610/864 to 599/864. These are output patterns, not identified stored states.

Single-copy effects are not cleanly selective. Under the swap mapping, first-copy transfer increases its affected first-query target from 82/432 to 94/432, while decreasing correctness on the nominally unchanged second query from 244/432 to 237/432. Second-copy transfer changes its affected second-query target from 188/432 to 192/432, while decreasing the unchanged first query from 350/432 to 347/432. Under the identity mapping, the single-copy affected-query targets remain 0/432 for either copy site; joint target adoption remains 0/864. Full instruction/order/mapping/query cells are retained.

The sensitivity blocks do not rescue the nominated account. Joint donor adoption changes from 270/1,728 to 271 at block 4 and 287 at block 12. Some single-site target effects are larger at block 12, but it was not the nominated primary block, and correctness/other-query tradeoffs remain. The complete matrix includes nearby, unrelated, wrong-version and three single/joint random controls; it should not be reduced to the largest selected effect. First-copy transfer has no dedicated first-site-only random control in this matrix; single-site random/spatial controls match the second-copy norm, while joint random controls match both component norms.

All software checks passed: 1,728/1,728 generation/replay first tokens agree; 6,912 identity shams preserve the output; all 22,464 final-block earlier-token interventions preserve it; and 1,728 final-answer-vector interventions copy the donor's actual first token. The latter is distinct from oracle correctness: imperfect donors yield only 1,458/1,728 correct donor answers. Maximum effective BF16 norm mismatch is 0.3454%, within the fixed 3% tolerance.

Raw residual-vector replacement changes an entire representation at one or two positions, not a verified single-variable code. Failed transfer also does not demonstrate that the value is absent: later positions can retain other paths from the original inputs. The supported result is a weak, nonselective effect in this particular candidate-site assay.

## Query timing with a common final cue

All 2,592 original timing prompts were replayed with exactly the original input IDs and crossed with an additional final query sentence, for 5,184 responses. Early/late pairs preserve their full token multiset, input length and all capture positions within each cue condition. The audit grouped cases into 3,888 distinct causal-prefix groups and checked 7,776 earlier-site/layer vector comparisons between members. All responses parsed.

| Prompt condition | Early correct | Late correct | Late minus early |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original timing cues, replayed | 1,030/1,296 | 1,199/1,296 | +13.04 percentage points |
| Identical final query in both | 1,191/1,296 | 1,213/1,296 | +1.70 percentage points |

The descriptive difference in differences is −11.34 percentage points. The final query helps the early condition by 161 correct responses and the late condition by 14. Paired early-only/late-only successes change from 48/217 without the final query to 65/87 with it.

Instruction effects remain heterogeneous. With the common final query, instructions 0, 1 and 2 score early/late 378/392, 392/405 and 421/416 out of 432 each. The swapped second-variable query still favors early cues in all three common-final-query instruction cells: 43/27, 51/46 and 51/39 out of 54. The small aggregate late advantage therefore does not describe every task cell.

Identical original prompts reproduce 2,560/2,592 earlier first tokens, with 32 differences under the changed batch/padding and execution profile. Their original outcomes remain preserved. The correct comparison is the new common-final versus original-cue contrast measured together in this run, rather than subtracting a historical score from a new one.

A recent, repeated query changes task instructions and query accessibility. The reduced timing gap shows that the old contrast is sensitive to this prompt factor; it does not isolate a retrieval, reconstruction or tracking algorithm, or measure a uniquely attributable recency effect.

## Consequences for the agenda

The separate [75-fit frequency-matched probe-control continuation](matched_probe_controls_decision.md) repairs the earlier two-label-versus-value-label mismatch and reproduces all original value predictions. Value recovery at the recorded answer-preparation site exceeds those controls. This strengthens the scoped decoding measurement, while leaving natural causal use and a general state representation unresolved.

The next scientific claims still require a reliable task regime and a selective, reproducible account before final forecasts are registered. More numeric variants of these familiar templates would not meet the unfamiliar-program prediction milestone. The relevant-live-load/computed regime, structural transfer and empirical frozen-prediction branches remain unfinished; optional language-model training, a separate explanation-transfer project and unlearning have not begun.
