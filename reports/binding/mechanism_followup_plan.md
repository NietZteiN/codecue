# Fresh numeric ownership and common-final-query controls

These two development studies follow the completed [timing and equivalent-copy study](timing_and_copy_decision.md). Their designs were saved before new model outcomes in `artifacts/binding/mechanism_followup/design_v1.json`. Both use the existing pinned Llama 3.1 8B Instruct checkpoint, native tokenizer, BF16 SDPA and the verified digit-aligned `Output:\n ` answer boundary.

The ownership study contains 2,592 prompts: 1,728 primary prompts plus 864 wrong-version donor controls. Its 36 ordered numeric pairs exclude both orientations of every pair from the earlier literal-transfer bank. Program templates, lexical forms and instructions remain familiar: this tests numeric novelty, not structural generalization. Every recipient has a donor reversing both initial values while preserving the full value multiset. Three instruction variants, two lexical forms, identity/swap mappings, both quantity queries and canonical/reordered assignments are retained.

Four sites are captured: the final definition of the first variable, the final definition of the second variable, the initial scratch literal and answer preparation. Definition positions come from the execution trace, including the relocated definition in wrong-version controls. Independent Python execution validates every baseline and every single/joint assignment-result counterfactual through the full trace.

The registered primary block is 8, selected from the earlier exploratory result. Blocks 4 and 12 measure sensitivity, and block 31 supplies analytic software controls. The 14 conditions are first-copy transfer, second-copy transfer, both-copy transfer, identity sham, nearby-position transfer, unrelated-position transfer, wrong-version direction, three single-site random seeds, three two-site random seeds and final-answer vector transfer. This gives 96,768 intervention forwards over 1,728 directed recipient/donor pairs. All cases remain in the primary analysis, including incorrect baselines.

Single-copy oracle substitutions change only that final variable; joint substitutions reproduce the complete donor state. Wrong-version directions are norm matched and their oracle labels describe assignment-result substitutions, without assuming that hidden vectors encode those labels. Spatial and single-site random controls match the second-copy perturbation; two-site random controls match both component norms separately. First-copy transfer has no dedicated first-site-only random control in this matrix. Actual norms are measured after BF16 rounding with the registered 3% tolerance. Each patched destination must exactly match its same-batch direct-forward replay. Identity shams, final-block earlier-token invariance and final-answer donor copying are mandatory checks.

The cue-control study crosses all 2,592 earlier timing prompts with the presence or absence of an identical final query sentence, giving 5,184 prompts and captures at blocks 8 and 31. The absent-cue condition must reproduce the exact original input token IDs. Within each cue condition, early/late pairs must preserve the full token multiset, length and all capture positions. The original outcomes are pinned for an explicit batch/hardware replay comparison. Accuracy cells, paired flips and the change in the late-minus-early gap are descriptive; repeating a recent query does not isolate a tracking or reconstruction algorithm.

The frozen inference source is recorded in `artifacts/binding/mechanism_followup/source_v1.json`; submitted jobs and their dependencies are in `jobs_v1.json` in the same directory. Both semantic/tokenizer preflights and all 228 regression tests passed before GPU inference. Both GPU jobs, their audits and complete-cell reports are now complete; see the [decision](mechanism_followup_decision.md). Resource accounting is uncapped, and all original final/extrapolation outcomes remain sealed.
