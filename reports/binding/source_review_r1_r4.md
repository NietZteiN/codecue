# R1–R4 primary-source and version review

Reviewed 2026-10-27, America/Chicago. All four supplied identifiers resolve. R1–R3 have verified conference records; R4 has an unresolved title inconsistency and no verified venue. Existing binding interventions, state-tracking diagnostics, and internal-readout monitoring are substantial precedents. None establishes our proposed binding/updating mechanism or converts our consumed-discovery confidence association into confirmation.

This review updates the [earlier comparison](related_work_comparison.md), checked against the [repository audit](repository_audit.md), rather than treating its descriptions as evidence. Full primary text and appendices were reviewed: R1 arXiv HTML, R2 the 30-page official conference PDF, and R3/R4 versioned arXiv HTML. Equations, tables and captions were inspected; figures were not independently digitized and code was not rerun. R1's linked proceedings PDF and R2's separate supplement returned browser-tool errors; these are access limitations, not evidence of 404s. Accessible full texts support the conclusions below. Machine-readable bibliography, dates and read scopes are in [the review record](../../artifacts/binding/literature/r1_r4_review_v1.json).

**R1 — Wu, Geiger and Millière, *How Do Transformers Learn Variable Binding in Symbolic Programs?*** Yiwei Wu, Atticus Geiger and Raphaël Millière are confirmed by the [ICML 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wu25j.html), PMLR 267:67284–67299. The [arXiv history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20896v2) records submission on 2025-05-27 and latest v2 on 2025-05-30.

The authors train a 37.8M-parameter transformer on 500,000 synthetic constant/copy programs, balancing one-to-four-hop queries with distractor chains. Root-value counterfactuals, residual/head interchange interventions, and selected-subspace swaps support a routing account that coexists with early-line shortcuts. Generalization has explicit limits: held-out variable/value combinations transfer, but length and deeper-chain results depend on the root's position; first-line shortcuts fail outside their training regime. Appendix J reports weak complete-state decoding despite successful execution. [Full text, §§2–3 and Appendices G–J](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20896v2).

For our study, neither a probe/behavior gap nor assignment-chain patching is a distinguishing result. Their trained model and dereferencing distribution do not establish selective reassignment-version interference in our pretrained checkpoints. Our output-confidence association does not identify their routing mechanism, or any alternative mechanism.

**R2 — Gur-Arieh, Geva and Geiger, *Mixing Mechanisms: How Language Models Retrieve Bound Entities In-Context*.** Yoav Gur-Arieh, Mor Geva and Atticus Geiger match the [ICLR 2026 record](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/2eeff35664016c7f0f8aa704f0d9a83e-Abstract-Conference.html). The [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182v2) dates v1 to 2025-10-07 and latest v2 to 2026-05-28; that revision date is not an independently established conference-PDF revision date.

Counterfactual entity-binding tasks make positional, lexical and reflexive retrieval predict different entities. Interchange interventions and attention knockouts test these accounts, including controls separating a reflexive pointer from an already-retrieved answer. Nine models cover two principal tasks; two models receive all ten tasks. The fitted mixture reaches approximately 0.95 Jensen–Shannon similarity on averaged intervention-conditioned entity distributions—not 95% answer accuracy. Filler-text and linguistic-variation tests extend scope, while heavy padding degrades signals. Appendix G separately controls sequence length when varying entity-group count. [Official full paper, §§3–5 and Appendices A–H](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/2eeff35664016c7f0f8aa704f0d9a83e-Paper-Conference.pdf).

This is direct precedent for competing retrieval explanations and quantitative causal-model evaluation. Our scalar confidence features neither separate these mechanisms nor measure assignment-version ownership. Their entity-group count also cannot be equated automatically with our available-bindings count or live output dependencies.

**R3 — Li, Guo and Andreas, *(How) Do Language Models Track State?*** Belinda Z. Li, Zifan Carl Guo and Jacob Andreas are verified in [ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25r.html), PMLR 267:34429–34452. The [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02854v3) lists v1 2025-03-04, v2 2025-03-11 and latest v3 2025-10-30. The reviewed v3 is later than the conference; PDF equivalence was not established.

Permutation-composition tasks, trained/fine-tuned GPT-2/Pythia models, state/parity probes, and prefix, suffix, substitution and deletion interventions distinguish associative and parity-associative signatures. Training curricula influence which signature emerges. Associative-type models generally learn faster and transfer farther by sequence length, but still encounter generalization cutoffs. The authors explicitly treat the signatures as necessary rather than sufficient conditions for idealized algorithms, and caution against transferring conclusions across state-tracking tasks. [Full text, §§2–6 and Appendices A–H](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02854v3).

This supports converging tests of competing algorithms, not unique mechanism identification from a probe or intervention curve. Our confidence predictor has no such algorithm-discriminating evidence. Grouped discovery prediction therefore cannot support a binding/updating account or unfamiliar-structure generalization by analogy to these permutation results.

**R4 — Chen and colleagues, arXiv:2606.17648.** The supplied [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17648v3) retains *From Brewing to Resolution: Tracing the Internal Lifecycle of Code Reasoning in LLMs*. However, the same v3's [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17648v3) and [PDF title page](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17648v3) say *From Brewing into Resolution: Tracing Internal Reasoning Trajectories in LLMs*. All twelve authors are recorded in the JSON. Versions are dated 2026-06-16, 2026-08-26 and 2026-10-11. This discrepancy remains unresolved; no venue was verified.

CUE-Bench spans six controlled Python families and sixteen models. Supervised layer-wise answer probes measure recoverability; context-stripped decoding transfers states into suffix-only runs, with baseline-logit subtraction, to assess readiness for producing an answer. Matched loop/unrolled and downstream intervention controls narrow causal interpretation. Appendix F's AUC 0.850 concerns Resolved-versus-other trajectories, not ordinary final correctness; some unresolved trajectories still answer correctly. Prediction-time label freedom does not eliminate supervised probe training or the layer-wise CSD target-forward cost. [Full text, §3 and Appendices C–G](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17648v3).

This is close precedent for CodeCue's recoverability/use distinction and monitoring. Our four output scalars use one target prefill, whereas CSD requires layer-wise interventions plus a baseline. Different access and labels prevent direct superiority claims. Neither our implementation differences nor our confidence signal establish novelty or rescue the unsupported original mechanism claim.

The defensible current position remains narrower: [our completed confidence analysis](discovery_confidence_decision.md) reports an exploratory failure-prediction association beyond fixed source baselines, with checkpoint-dependent strength and conditional uncertainty. It does not establish causal binding interference, complete-answer confidence, or performance on untouched structures. This selected review supports those limits; it is not an exhaustive novelty search or a priority claim.
