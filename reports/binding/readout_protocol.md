# Discovery readouts and measurement controls

The first capture bank and all 9,216 matched Transformers responses passed the tensor/metadata audit. Raw blocks 8, 16 and 24 were measured before the query and at the end of its print line. Before-query duplicates have exactly equal vectors and are deduplicated. These sites do not include the final assistant answer-prefill boundary.

The primary readout has two 32-class weighted ridge heads for current and saved bindings. It trains on neutral training cases only, with train-only normalization and fixed regularization 100. Seeds 11, 29 and 47 draw positive weights over training structural components, retaining all structures and value classes. They are not independent experimental replications. Labels are the oracle bindings visible at each prefix, not generated answers.

Hidden and character-ngram surface features each have 4,096 dimensions and identical output-head capacity. Surface hashing learns no development vocabulary. Identifier-control labels are random functions of slot and lexical stem, removing the train/dev prefix. Unseen-stem coverage is explicit; selectivity is not reported on unsupported identifiers. All seed-level class support, parameter counts and control metrics are saved. A post-hoc difficulty audit (`artifacts/binding/state_readouts/control_entropy_audit_v1.json`) finds only two control labels per canonical head, with 1 bit of entropy, versus 32 value labels with 4.81–4.89 bits. Matching feature and head capacity did not match target difficulty. Higher identifier-control accuracy and the recorded negative accuracy differences cannot decisively reject value recovery or establish semantic selectivity.

The primary [readouts](state_readouts.md) recover current values at about 53% at the before-query block-24 site. A post-hoc diagnostic separates about 99% recovery when the current literal ends that prefix from only 6–8% after dead scratch statements. This boundary dependence limits a general register-like interpretation. Saved-value recovery is weak despite strong saved-answer behavior. Failed decoding here cannot establish information absence, and the easier identifier controls cannot resolve selectivity.

The follow-up preserves all primary fits and adds a pre-answer site at the final input token and raw block 31. The first prefill-logit argmax must equal the generated first token in every case. This frozen next-token consistency check does not establish that a complete multi-token integer is linearly encoded.

Both the [original canonical heads](state_readouts_v2_all_slots.md) and a separately scoped [queried-value head](state_readouts_v2_queried_value.md) have completed. The latter runs only after the query is visible, never using future query identity before the query. It asks a different measurement question and has fewer parameters, with its own surface/identifier controls. All old sites were remeasured on the same A30 alongside the new sites.

The second capture kept the full cohort, BF16 precision, batch size and greedy protocol. All 9,216 next-token checks and the complete tensor/score audit passed before fitting. At raw block 31, queried-value accuracy on neutral development cases increased from 6.4% at the query-line boundary to 87.9% at answer preparation, averaged across the three fit-weighting seeds. The corresponding pre-answer surface head reached 9.1%. These are conditional recovery measurements at different sites, not evidence of causal use or a global binding dictionary.

Current source/jobs: `artifacts/binding/state_capture/source_v2.json` and `jobs_v2.json`; every job is complete. The measured 16.3-GiB peak made A30/H100/H200 eligible, with no project compute cap. No causal mechanism, selective intervention, final-test prediction or language-model training is claimed. A controlled intervention assay remains separate.
